# Should Strata switch? The 2026-11-23 read of three systems

**Question (owner, 2026-11-23):** agentic-graphrag vs Hindsight vs Obsidian vs what we built — or
take from each? And specifically: switch, port the memories worth keeping, delete the rest. Named
weakness to weigh: **the graphing pass, and how long it takes.**

**Answer in one line.** Keep ours and take eighteen things; the switch costs a two-day GPU lockout to buy
three rungs we do not have and lose the tiers none of them has.

**This is a recommendation, not a measurement of the alternatives on our data.** No arm ran our
3,819 facts through Hindsight's `retain()`. The port cost below is arithmetic on our own measured
decode rate, labeled as such.

---

## 1. What the graph pass actually costs, and what fixes it

Measured on this box, 2026-11-23:

| the number | value |
|---|---|
| the last full pass | **3,187 s** (53 min), 1,079,123 tokens in / 323,288 out, 26 chunks, model = the served one |
| facts it read | 1,183, of 3,819 active facts — **31% coverage**, 612 rows queued behind it |
| what the arm can walk | **1,020 of 3,133 edges (33%)** — `fid()` drops any edge with a concept on one end |
| output per fact | **273 tokens** (323,288 ÷ 1,183) → 2.7 s of decode per fact at this box's ~90 tok/s |
| the store's growth | 150–234 facts/day (last 14 days), so the queue refills in ~2.6 days |
| a full-store pass at that rate | ~103 min of GPU, **every ~2.6 days** |
| the cheapest alternative | **a k=8 vector graph over all 3,755 facts: 0.423 s, 15,020 edges, 0 tokens** (measured, same box, `serve.memory._vec_matrix`) |

The pass is **decode-bound**, which the module's own docstring already says: "a pass is bound by
OUTPUT, not input". So the lever is output tokens per fact, not batch size. Chunks of 4 files are
already tuned to the 16,384-token ceiling.

The fix is not one faster pass. It is **three layers at three prices**, which is what the code
wants anyway (the read path already fuses two edge sources and ignores a third):

| layer | cost | what it buys | freshness |
|---|---|---|---|
| typed `links` in Postgres — 858 rows today, **all `kind='about'`** | 0 tokens, 0 s | the relations a human chose, with a note | instant |
| vector kNN, the `graph_sim_k` layer generalized | **0.423 s, 0 tokens** | 15,020 edges, 100% of facts | every write |
| the LLM concept pass | 3,187 s, 1.4M tokens | **1,913 concept nodes** — abstractions no single row carries | every few days |

Two of the three are already built. The work is (a) walking the concept nodes instead of dropping
67% of the edges, (b) letting the typed edges vote, (c) making the vector layer **standing** —
materialized on write, the way Hindsight does it (`memories/pg/link_expansion.py` reads a kNN graph
written at insert, so its read path pays no rebuild) — so the LLM layer stops being the graph's
spine and becomes its slow top coat.

On this store's scale the standing layer is nearly free: `serve/memory.py`'s own docstring already
argues it — *"At this store's scale a full cosine scan is the honest design: ~1 ms per 2.5k rows
measured at build, so an ANN index would be more machinery than math; a 100k-row store is the point
to revisit."* Measured today: 0.113 ms per fact for k=8. At 100k rows that is 11 s, which is the
number that tells us when to revisit.

Worth naming: 1,608 communities over 2,136 rows is **1.33 rows per community** — the community layer
currently consolidates nothing, so agentic-graphrag's ≥2-entities floor and support-weighted edges
are the cheap correction.

## 2. The port, priced

Our store today: 3,819 active facts, 6,684 retired rows in supersession chains, 33,475 doc chunks
(140 books), 858 typed links, 43,329 journal turns, 91 shelves with `MEM/CAT` labels, six persona
files (~120,049 chars) in a 720 MB standing cart.

**Hindsight** (Postgres + pgvector, MIT — the same storage class, so the schema port is the cheap
part):

- Facts: 3,819 `retain()` calls. Their retain runs an LLM extraction over each item (facts, temporal
  data, entities, relationships, then normalization). Arithmetic on our measured 273 out-tokens/fact
  at ~90 tok/s: **≈ 3.2 hours of this GPU**.
- Chunks: 33,475 items → **≈ 28 hours**. Turns as episodes: 43,329 → ≈ 131 hours.
- Practical port: facts + the founding corpus, chunks re-cut by their loader (Docling-style typed
  structure is a genuine gain), turns left in place as the journal.
- **Two-day GPU lockout** for the honest version. The GPU is the one serving this conversation.

**agentic-graphrag**: Neo4j or FalkorDB, plus optional Qdrant/Milvus/Weaviate — a second and third
database against the filter this directory has always applied. Ingestion with GLiNER is CPU work, so
the corpus port is cheap; the schema has to be authored (nine entity types, eight relations in the
default preset, `operations` and `legal` presets beside it) before the graph means anything.

**Obsidian**: not a replacement — a **projection**. Measured on this box with our own vendored
exporter: a graph of 212,916 notes / 91.0 MB of text / 888 MB on disk in one command. Our facts
alone are ~2,106 notes ≈ 8.6 MB. Nothing is deleted, nothing is re-ingested, and the retrieval stack
stays where it is.

Items 1-2 are the graph answer. Items 3-7 are the layered shape taken as rungs rather than as a
migration — and **3 and 4 are the two cheapest items on the whole page**. Items 8-12 make the harvest
trustworthy. Items 13-14 make the store livable. **Item 15 is the one that pays for itself the first time
the corpus changes**: the extraction-fingerprint split means a re-cut of the shelf taxonomy no longer has
to re-embed 33,475 rows.

## 4. What we keep by staying

Four things none of the three has, all measured on this box:

1. **The tier ladder.** A 720 MB standing cart of persona bytes restored in ~130 ms, so the
   standing 30k tokens cost no prefill; a per-turn block that keeps the head stable and the tail
   fresh; a browse tool the model calls inside the request on all three dialects. Hindsight's recall
   returns text a client resends (its own default budget is 4,096 tokens); agentic-graphrag hands
   tools to an external agent.
2. **One store, and a lexical arm that is what it says.** Postgres, embedded cluster, the vectors
   inside, the backups encrypted (`SDMP1`, restore verified byte-identical). agentic-graphrag: Neo4j
   or FalkorDB plus an optional vector DB. Hindsight: Postgres + pgvector — the closest of the three,
   a peer on this axis, and its default lexical arm is `ts_rank_cd`, documented in its own docs as
   **"(TF-IDF, not true BM25)"**. Ours is true BM25 with family-scoped idf, chosen on 2026-10-16 when
   `ts_rank_cd` measured top1 0.394 → 0.186.
3. **A confidence column that ranks** (theirs was dropped from the shipped schema), **a supersession
   chain that names what it replaced, per row, forever** (theirs is 3 bracketed states inline plus a
   50-deep snapshot table), and **shelves as an address you can read**: `catalog:true` is a librarian
   reading the index; a bank list is a partition list, and its observations tree is a folder of
   markdown. The vault takes this further, not away.
4. **Instruments.** The frozen 12-topic panel (PAPER/SHELF/FILE/MRR), `lme_retrieval_ab --offline`
   (~45 s), the venue (~80 min), temp 0.8/1.0 with ≥3 repeats, a muted control that answered `idk`
   1,478/1,500. Every vendor number in this directory, including Hindsight's 91.4%, is a statement
   about a reader model as much as a memory system. Their eval suite (`hindsight-system-evals`, ~100
   category-labelled judged cases with per-model pass rates and the failures explained) is the same
   discipline — which is the strongest argument on their side, and the reason our panel is an early
   asset rather than a unique one.

## 5. The honest case for switching

State it at full strength, since the decision is the owner's and he should overrule knowing what he
overrules:

- Hindsight is the most-adopted system in the field (~62,900 stars, MIT, Postgres-native, 78 releases,
  ~291 contributors) and its shape names exactly the rungs we lack: observations, entity summaries,
  mental models. Adopting it buys all three **at once, working, with a UI and 48 integrations**,
  instead of six projects here. Its write path is also better shaped than ours on one axis worth
  naming: **one LLM call per 3,000 chars, then everything deterministic** — entity resolution is
  spaCy + `pg_trgm` + union-find with no model at all, and reads cost zero LLM calls.
- Its published numbers are far above our 0.761: **83.6% vs 39.0%** full-context on the same 20B
  backbone, **91.4%** on LongMemEval scaled.
- It would delete our hardest maintenance surface: the 40-knob constructor seam, the 41 `m4_*`
  scripts, and the clerk's unmeasured labeling.
- **The counter-weight, now read in code and the paper's own tables.** The 91.4% row is Hindsight's
  memory stack on OSS-120B with **Gemini-3 Pro as the answer generator** — a frontier model on top;
  the apples-to-apples row is **89.0%** on OSS-120B. The judge is **GPT-OSS-120B at temp 0.0, their
  own**. The "independent" reproductions are by institutions **on the author list** (Virginia Tech,
  The Washington Post). Paper §7.3 ships with literal **`<add>`** placeholders for the retrieval token
  budgets — the variable that most moves the score. Their benchmark site now reads **94.6%**, above
  the paper, with no paper behind it, on **their own** leaderboard domain. The shipped schema has
  **three** layers, not the paper's four: the opinion network and its `confidence_score` column were
  dropped. And the 221 open issues name the failure modes of exactly the machinery we lack: #5574
  delta pages grow to 3-4× their budget because *"the budget is a prompt hint only and nothing
  enforces it"*; #6600 reflect times out contending with consolidation; #6241 append retains rewrite
  every prior unit each turn.
- And the decisive one for us: our own residual (244 of 346 wrong lines had every gold session
  present) says the **reader**, not the store, is where that headroom lives — so a switch spends a
  two-day port on a store swap, and keeps the same ceiling.

## 6. The decision I'd make

**Stay, and take items 1-4 this cycle.** The graph fix (1-2) removes the named weakness for ~50 lines
and a panel run, and turns the 53-minute pass from the graph's spine into its top coat. The time mark
(3) and the three budgets (4) are the two cheapest structural wins on the page — a boolean against a
column we already store, and splitting one knob into three — and both attack the two failures this
project has measured and never fixed: temporal at 0.465, and a block budget spent on this afternoon's
sentences. The observation rung (5) is the biggest structural gain from outside and is one row type.
Items 6-14 ride the next cycle.

One line on why the port is not the cheap option, stated as arithmetic and not as attachment: the
port costs ~3.2 h of this GPU for the facts and ~28 h for the corpus, and what it buys on the far side
is a store whose read path is faster (zero LLM calls, a local 114M reranker) and whose write path is
slower (one LLM call per 3,000 chars, against our model-free `remember()` at 2.8 ms for a guarded
write), on a schema that has been renamed twice in ten months and ships 221 open issues.

**Re-open the switch question when one of these fires** — the falsifier, stated before the run, so
this page ages honestly:

- the panel's MRR does not move after items 1-2 ship, meaning the graph's value was never in its
  edges;
- the observation rung's consolidation ratio is flat after a month of the sweep, meaning we do not
  know how to consolidate;
- the clerk's label quality stays unmeasured at the next audit, meaning the harvest is a habit and
  not an instrument.
